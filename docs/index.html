Abstract

With the proliferation of speech AI agents, understanding emotional entrainment in conversational interaction has become increasingly important. Emotional entrainment is shaped by social relationships and conversational context, influencing affective coordination over time. We introduce DyadEE, a dataset containing emotionally entrained conversations and controlled interactions where entrainment is disrupted through emotion resynthesis. We further propose TRACE, a window-level framework that models dyadic interaction as ordered sequences of acoustic embeddings derived from emotion-finetuned Whisper representations. TRACE compares late-fusion and cross-attention strategies for incorporating relationship and context signals into temporal modeling. On DyadEE, relationship and context information improve emotional entrainment detection, with TRACE-CE achieving 93.47% accuracy under speech + relationship conditioning.

Overview

TRACE studies emotional entrainment as a temporally evolving, socially grounded property of dyadic speech—not simply a property of isolated utterances.

Research questions. (RQ1) How can emotional entrainment be disrupted in a controlled way while limiting trivial acoustic shortcuts? (RQ2) How should conversational context and social relationship be integrated with temporal speech representations for entrainment detection?
DyadEEA controlled benchmark derived from Seamless Interaction with emotionally entrained and deliberately disrupted dyads.
TRACE-LFLate-fusion architecture that combines temporal speech representations with contextual and relationship embeddings.
TRACE-CECross-attention architecture that allows temporal speech states to condition on social information.

Method

TRACE preserves the ordered interaction between speakers. Audio is segmented into aligned windows, encoded with affect-aware speech representations, arranged into a dyadic temporal sequence, and then conditioned on context and relationship.

Step 1Two-speaker audioDyadic conversations from Seamless Interaction.
Step 23 s windowsAligned window-level observations preserve temporal evolution.
Step 3Affect embeddingsEmotion-finetuned Whisper-large-v3 representations.
Step 4Temporal modelingOrdered speaker interaction modeled by TRACE-LF or TRACE-CE.
Step 5Social conditioningConversational context and relationship embeddings.

DyadEE: Dyadic Emotional Entrainment Dataset

DyadEE contains 8,434 dyads spanning seven relationship categories and twenty manually reviewed conversational context categories. Controlled emotion resynthesis creates non-entrained interactions, while voice conversion is used to reduce dependence on synthesis artifacts as a label shortcut.

Controlled benchmark framing. DyadEE's non-entrained conditions are generated through controlled transformations. They enable targeted evaluation of disrupted emotional coordination, but do not exhaust naturally occurring non-entrainment in real conversations.

Architecture-specific conditioning

TRACE-LF benefits most from conversational context (83.59 → 88.71), while TRACE-CE benefits most from relationship conditioning (86.82 → 93.47).

Human validation

Listeners assign substantially higher entrainment scores to EE than controlled NE dyads, supporting the benchmark labels as perceptually meaningful.

Limitations

TRACE operates on audio-only input and DyadEE uses controlled synthetic transformations for the non-entrained condition. Generalization to naturally occurring disengagement or affective mismatch remains open. The benchmark is currently English-only, relationship/context embeddings may encode culture-specific emotional norms, and binary labels do not represent the graded and time-varying nature of affective coordination.
