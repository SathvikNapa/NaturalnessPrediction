Abstract

With the proliferation of speech AI agents, understanding emotional entrainment in conversational interaction has become increasingly important. Emotional entrainment is shaped by social relationships and conversational context, influencing affective coordination over time. We introduce DyadEE, a dataset containing emotionally entrained conversations and controlled interactions where entrainment is disrupted through emotion resynthesis. We further propose TRACE, a window-level framework that models dyadic interaction as ordered sequences of acoustic embeddings derived from emotion-finetuned Whisper representations. TRACE compares late-fusion and cross-attention strategies for incorporating relationship and context signals into temporal modeling. On DyadEE, relationship and context information improve emotional entrainment detection, with TRACE-CE achieving 93.47% accuracy under speech + relationship conditioning.

Overview

TRACE studies emotional entrainment as a temporally evolving and socially grounded property of dyadic speech, rather than a property of isolated utterances.

Research Questions

  1. RQ1. How can emotional entrainment be disrupted in a controlled way while limiting trivial acoustic shortcuts?
  2. RQ2. How should conversational context and social relationship be integrated with temporal speech representations for entrainment detection?

DyadEE

A controlled benchmark derived from Seamless Interaction with emotionally entrained and deliberately disrupted dyads.

TRACE-LF

A late-fusion architecture that combines temporal speech representations with contextual and relationship embeddings.

TRACE-CE

A cross-attention architecture that conditions temporal speech states on social information.

DyadEE Dataset

DyadEE derives emotionally entrained and controlled non-entrained dyads from Seamless Interaction, tagging each pair with a relationship category and a high-level conversational context.

Method

TRACE turns a paired-speaker dyad into a temporally ordered sequence of affect-aware embeddings, encodes it with a shared bidirectional LLaMA-style trunk, then fuses relationship and context signals either by late fusion (TRACE-LF) or by cross-attention (TRACE-CE).

Architecture-specific conditioning

TRACE-LF benefits most from conversational context (83.59 → 88.71), while TRACE-CE benefits most from relationship conditioning (86.82 → 93.47).

Human validation

Listeners assign substantially higher entrainment scores to EE than controlled NE dyads, supporting the benchmark labels as perceptually meaningful.

Limitations

TRACE operates on audio-only input and DyadEE uses controlled synthetic transformations for the non-entrained condition. Generalization to naturally occurring disengagement or affective mismatch remains open. The benchmark is currently English-only, relationship/context embeddings may encode culture-specific emotional norms, and binary labels do not represent the graded and time-varying nature of affective coordination.
